# Aptitude bootcamp for absolute beginners

Aptitude and analytical reasoning tests measure your ability to think logically and solve quantitative problems.

If math formulas scare you, it is because you have been memorizing numbers instead of visualizing relationships. Let's build a physical, visual mental model of the core concepts.

---

## 1. Percentages and Fractions: The Pizza Slices

A percentage is simply a fraction where the denominator is always **`100`**.

Imagine a large **pizza** cut into slices:
* If the pizza is cut into **4 equal slices**, and you eat 1 slice, you ate $\frac{1}{4}$ of the pizza.
* To turn $\frac{1}{4}$ into a percentage, you scale the bottom number to `100`:
  $$\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1 \times 25}{4 \times 25} = \frac{25}{100} = 25\%$$
* If the pizza has 8 slices, and you eat 2, you still ate $\frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4} = 25\%$.
* In aptitude questions, always reduce percentages back to simple, clean fractions first. For example, memorizing that $33.33\% = \frac{1}{3}$ or $12.5\% = \frac{1}{8}$ makes complex arithmetic immediate.

---

## 2. Ratios: The Paint Mixer

A ratio is a way of comparing the relative sizes of different groups.

Imagine mixing paint in a bucket:
* You mix **3 cups of blue paint** with **2 cups of yellow paint** to make green.
* The ratio of Blue to Yellow is **`3:2`**.
* This means the entire mixture has $3 + 2 = 5$ total parts.
* Blue paint represents $\frac{3}{5}$ ($60\%$) of the mixture.
* Yellow paint represents $\frac{2}{5}$ ($40\%$) of the mixture.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> If you have 20 liters of this green paint, you can find how much blue paint is in it by dividing the total into 5 equal parts: $20 \div 5 = 4$ liters per part. Since blue is 3 parts, you have $3 \times 4 = 12$ liters of blue paint.

---

## 3. Probability: The Bag of Marbles

Probability is simply counting: **`what you want` divided by `every possible outcome`**.

Imagine a bag containing **3 red marbles** and **2 blue marbles**.
* Total possible outcomes: $3 + 2 = 5$ marbles.
* If you close your eyes and draw one marble, the probability of getting a red marble is:
  $$\text{Probability} = \frac{\text{Wanted Outcomes}}{\text{Total Outcomes}} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6 \text{ (or } 60\%)$$

---

## 4. Venn Diagrams: The Social Circles

Venn diagrams group items sharing traits.

Imagine a group of 10 students:
* **6 students** play cricket.
* **5 students** play football.
* If you add them together, $6 + 5 = 11$, which is more than the 10 students in the class!
* This means there must be an overlap. At least $11 - 10 = 1$ student plays **both** sports.

```text
       CRICKET (6)          FOOTBALL (5)
      +-----------+--------+-----------+
      |  Cricket  |  Both  | Football  |
      |   Only    |  (1)   |   Only    |
      |    (5)    |        |    (4)    |
      +-----------+--------+-----------+
```

## 5. Review Exercises
1. If an item is on sale with a $20\%$ discount, what fraction of the original price do you pay?
2. A bucket of juice contains apple and orange juice in the ratio $4:1$. If there is 15 liters of juice in total, how many liters are orange?
3. If you roll a standard 6-sided die, what is the probability of rolling a number greater than 4?
